The Architect of Ithaca

The Man Odysseus Erased

Everyone knows Odysseus's story. This is the story of the man he destroyed to keep it.

Palamedes of Euboea — inventor of the alphabet the army counts with, the lighthouses it sails by, and the dice that keep it sane — makes one mistake: he drags Odysseus, feigning madness behind a plough, into the war he tried to escape.

Nine years later comes a forged letter, a rigged trial, a pit of stones — and a poem that never once says his name.

The Architect of Ithaca is a literary retelling of the man the Odyssey could not afford to remember: precise, unsentimental, and quietly furious on his behalf. Includes the afterword "The Psychology of Palamedes." The sixth book in the Myths of the Ancient World series. Reads as a complete standalone. For readers of Madeline Miller, Claire North's Ithaca, and Stephen Fry.

Also available in Spanish.

"He built the trick that won the war. He was owed a name for it. He got a pit of stones instead."

What You'll Find Inside

Key Themes

  • * The inventor and the war he made possible: Palamedes gives the Greek army letters, numbers, dice, and lighthouses — and the one thing it actually asked for: Odysseus.
  • * The debt a clever man never forgives: Odysseus's feigned madness, exposed in front of every king in Greece — and what a man like that does with nine years to plan a repayment
  • * A forged letter, planted gold, a pit of stones: How a rigged trial actually worked in the Greek camp before Troy — and why the whole army agreed to believe it
  • * What the poem chose to forget: The Odyssey never says his name. This book asks what it cost the story to leave him out of it.
